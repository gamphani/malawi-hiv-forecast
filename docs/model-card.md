# Model card: district HIV new-diagnosis forecaster (v0.1, SYNTHETIC DATA ONLY)

A model card is a short, standard "label on the box": what the model is, what it is for, what it is
not for, how well it works and for whom. Read this before using any forecast.

## 1. What it is
- **Task:** forecast monthly new HIV diagnoses for each district, 1 to 6 months ahead, with 80% and 95% ranges.
- **Model:** one pooled gradient-boosted model (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) trained
  across all districts and horizons. It fits five quantiles (2.5%, 10%, 50%, 90%, 97.5%); the median is the forecast.
- **Calibration:** conformalized quantile regression (CQR). Ranges are widened by an amount learned from the
  model's own errors on the latest 12 forecast origins.
- **Inputs (14):** recent diagnoses (lags and rolling means), same month last year, month of year,
  district size and level, urban/rural, and four testing-related features. Counts are scaled by each district's level.
- **Explanations:** SHAP values show which inputs pushed a forecast up or down.

## 2. Intended use
- Support district-level planning conversations (for example, where to look first), next to local knowledge.
- Not for: allocating money or staff on its own, individual-level decisions, or any place or time not in the training data.
- Intended users: programme planners and analysts, once validated on real data.

## 3. Data
- **Synthetic only.** 28 districts x 72 months (2,016 rows), generated by `src/generate_data.py` with seed 42.
  Patterns (seasonality, testing, campaigns, missing reports) were built in by us. See `docs/data-card-synthetic.md`.
- Nothing here describes Malawi. Real routine data have not been accessed.

## 4. How it was evaluated
- **Rolling origin:** repeatedly pretend it is month t, fit on the past only, forecast t+1 to t+6, compare with what happened,
  then move forward 3 months (first origin month 36). This mimics real use and avoids looking into the future (a test checks the features do not leak).
- **Metrics:** MASE (error scaled by a repeat-last-month forecast; 1.0 is about as good as that, lower is better);
  interval coverage (share of outcomes inside the range); WIS (weighted interval score, rewards ranges that are narrow and right).
- **Baselines:** repeat last month (naive), repeat same month last year (seasonal naive), Holt-Winters exponential smoothing.

## 5. Results (synthetic)
| Model | MASE overall |
|---|---|
| Naive | 1.100 |
| Seasonal naive | 1.006 |
| Exponential smoothing (best baseline) | 0.855 |
| Gradient boosting | 0.755 |
| Gradient boosting without testing inputs | 0.751 |

- Gradient boosting beat the best baseline by about 12%. Hiding the testing inputs changed nothing, so the gain is **not** from testing information.
- **By district size (gbm MASE):** large 0.68, medium 0.74, small 0.82. Small districts are harder.
- **Campaign months:** 1.28 versus 0.72 in normal months. Campaigns are not predicted and break the model.
- **Interval honesty (coverage, target 0.80 / 0.95):**

| | Raw 80% | Calibrated 80% | Raw 95% | Calibrated 95% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All months | 0.747 | 0.799 | 0.915 | 0.943 |
| Campaign months | 0.50 | 0.561 | 0.786 | 0.816 |
| Small districts | n/a | 0.768 | n/a | 0.909 |

Calibration fixes the average; it cannot foresee campaigns or fully fix small districts.
Full tables: `docs/results.md`.

## 6. Limitations and risks
1. Synthetic data: results are optimistic and say nothing about Malawi.
2. The target is diagnoses, a proxy for infections. It rises and falls with testing effort.
3. Campaigns are unknown to the model and cause the biggest misses and under-covered ranges.
4. Small and rural districts are less reliable; counts are low and reports more often missing.
5. SHAP shows what the model **uses**, not what is true or what a district **needs**.
6. No tuning was done; settings were chosen once. Forecasts from one district are correlated, so effective sample sizes are smaller than they look.
7. Equity: results are reported by urban/rural and size, but a model that is worse for small rural districts can steer attention away from them if used carelessly.

## 7. Before real use (not done yet)
Research clearance and data-sharing agreement; re-run on real series; check calibration by group; user testing with planners; a named owner and update plan; monitor accuracy after launch.

## 8. Reproduce
`pip install -r requirements.txt`; `python src/generate_data.py`; `pytest -q`; `python src/report.py`; `streamlit run app.py`.
