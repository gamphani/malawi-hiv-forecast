# Explainable machine-learning forecasts of district HIV new diagnoses: a synthetic-data prototype

Dave Phiri, Africa CDC (AES-PHI fellowship). Prototype v0.1. **All results use synthetic data and say nothing about Malawi.**

*Reporting note.* Structure follows the spirit of TRIPOD+AI (Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model
for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis, extended for AI methods), a checklist for reporting prediction models. TRIPOD+AI was
written mainly for individual-level models, so some items are adapted to a district time series; it is a guide here, not a claim of full compliance.

## Abstract
**Background.** Planners need district forecasts of new HIV diagnoses with honest uncertainty. **Objective.** Build and test,
on synthetic data, a pipeline that beats simple baselines, states calibrated uncertainty and explains itself.
**Methods.** 28 synthetic districts x 72 months. Rolling-origin evaluation (horizons 1 to 6 months) compared naive, seasonal naive
and exponential smoothing with quantile gradient boosting, calibrated by conformalized quantile regression. SHAP explained forecasts.
**Results.** MASE: exponential smoothing 0.855, gradient boosting 0.755 (about 12% better). Hiding testing inputs left accuracy unchanged (0.751).
Calibration moved 80% interval coverage from 0.747 to 0.799 and 95% from 0.915 to 0.943, but campaign months stayed under-covered (0.561 and 0.816).
**Conclusion.** The workflow works end to end. Whether machine learning helps for Malawi can only be answered on real data.

## 1. Introduction
Diagnoses are recorded; infections are not observed. We forecast diagnoses as a proxy, knowing they depend on testing effort.
The question is practical: can a transparent machine-learning model beat simple methods, say how unsure it is, and be used by planners?

## 2. Methods
**Source of data.** Generated by `src/generate_data.py` (seed 42) with seasonality, district size, urban/rural differences, testing,
campaign months and 2 to 6% missing reports. Real data not accessed.
**Outcome.** Monthly new diagnoses per district; forecast 1 to 6 months ahead.
**Predictors.** 14 features from the past only: lags, rolling means, same month last year, month, district size and level, urban/rural, testing features.
Missing months were filled for feature building only.
**Model.** One pooled `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` per quantile (2.5, 10, 50, 90, 97.5%). Median = forecast.
**Calibration.** Conformalized quantile regression using the latest 12 forecast origins.
**Comparators.** Naive, seasonal naive, Holt-Winters.
**Validation.** Rolling origin (first origin month 36, step 3), refitting at each origin. Metrics: MASE, coverage, WIS. A unit test checks that no feature uses future data.
**Subgroups.** Urban/rural, district size, campaign months.
**Explainability.** SHAP (Shapley additive explanations), checked for additivity (gap 0.0000).
**Ablation.** Testing features hidden.

## 3. Results
| Model | MASE |
|---|---|
| Naive | 1.100 |
| Seasonal naive | 1.006 |
| Exponential smoothing | 0.855 |
| Gradient boosting | 0.755 |
| Gradient boosting, no testing inputs | 0.751 |

By size (gbm): large 0.68, medium 0.74, small 0.82. Campaign months 1.28 versus 0.72 in normal months.
Coverage before and after calibration, overall: 80% 0.747 to 0.799; 95% 0.915 to 0.943. Small districts after calibration 0.768 and 0.909.
Weighted interval score unchanged (0.331 to 0.330): calibration corrected honesty without making ranges more useful.
SHAP: leading drivers were month of year, district level, relative size and the same month last year.

## 4. Discussion
**Interpretation.** The gain over exponential smoothing is modest but consistent. Because removing testing inputs did not hurt, the gain comes from seasonality and level, which we built into the data; real data may differ.
**Explanations are not needs.** SHAP describes what the model uses. It does not show what a district needs or why cases changed.
**Limitations.** Synthetic data; proxy outcome; unknown campaigns; weaker small and rural districts; no tuning; correlated forecasts.
**Equity.** Gaps are reported by group so they are visible rather than averaged away.
**Next steps.** Obtain real aggregate series under an agreement and research clearance; repeat evaluation; user-test the dashboard with planners; agree owner and update plan.

## Glossary
- **MASE:** error divided by the error of repeating last month. Below 1 beats that simple rule.
- **Coverage:** share of real outcomes inside a stated range. An honest 80% range should hold about 80%.
- **WIS:** one score that rewards ranges that are both right and narrow.
- **Conformal calibration:** adjusting ranges using recent errors so they hold their promised share.
- **SHAP:** a way to split a forecast into contributions from each input.
- **Ablation:** removing a part to see whether it mattered.
- **Rolling origin:** testing repeatedly as if at different past dates, using only earlier data.
